Why patient bakers end up with better bread

The Weekend Table · 6 min read
Article photo

Most home bakers rush the process. They knead, wait an hour, and bake. But the bakers who get the most flavor out of their loaves tend to do the opposite: they slow everything down and let time do the heavy lifting.

When the mixture ferments slowly in a cool place, the yeast and bacteria have more time to break down starches. The result is a loaf with a deeper, slightly tangy taste and a crust that crackles as it cools.

It can feel tedious at first. You have to plan a day ahead, and there is no way to speed things up once you have started. Still, once you figure out a schedule that fits your week, the extra hours mostly take care of themselves.

If you are starting from scratch, begin with a simple recipe and change one thing at a time. Keep notes on how long it rested and how warm the kitchen was, so you can trace each rise and fall back to its cause.

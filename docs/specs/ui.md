# UI spec (follows the mock)

The [UI mock](https://claude.ai/artifact/SMxhVni5DdE6cK8UazvqGS) is canonical: when it and this file disagree, the mock wins, and this file is updated to match. Artboards: `YouTube — extension ON`, `YouTube — comments`, `Web article — highlights and phrase saving`, `Opening Langle from the browser toolbar`, `Settings page`, `Word list page`, `Phase 2 — …` (words in this video, fullscreen, past occurrences), `Words on this page (article)` (`ArticleWords`), `LanguageData`.

## Shared: highlights
- Categories and looks: **Learning** = pale green background + green underline, **New** = pale blue background + blue underline, **Known** = gray underline
- Each category can be turned on/off; **by default only Learning is on**
- When subtitles are drawn over the video, the same category colors are brightened so they read on dark backgrounds
- Inflected forms are matched to the base form (ferments → ferment, loaves → loaf)

## Shared: theme
- Settings → Appearance → Theme: **System** (default; follows the browser's light or dark mode through `prefers-color-scheme`) / Light / Dark
- Applies to everything Langle draws: toolbar popup, settings page, word list, and the on-page UI (hover card, also over a selection, dictionary window and panel, video panel and transcript, the ^ menu, the below-video subtitle box)
- Not themed: subtitles drawn over the video and the L / ^ player buttons (already for a dark video), and the highlights painted into a website's own text (the page keeps its own colors)

## Shared: hover card
- Kind (Word / Phrase) and current status. Shown for every word in the vocabulary, also those known by their frequency (within the known level, not saved): they show as **Known**, with "Known" pressed; pressing it saves them as Known, "+ Learning" as Learning. Not for a name (user, 2026-11-13; [architecture](architecture.md) "Names and written case"): a word the page or the video always capitalises inside sentences that is a person's name gets no highlight and no card, like a number. A name with a meaning of its own stays a word and its card shows that meaning first (France → フランス, June → 6月, Japan → 日本)
- A word capitalised inside a sentence shows its capitalised headword's meanings first (Japan → 日本, US → 合衆国); in lower case, only the lowercase headword's ("oh" never shows Ohio)
- Headword, **IPA (always shown when available)**, short translation, extra notes (etymology, related words, pronunciation notes)
- **Up to three senses** (2026-11-13; mock `Web article`, `YouTube — extension ON`, `Fullscreen video`): the short translation is the entry's first senses in dictionary order, one per line, stacked, all alike (15 px, the meaning color, no numbers or bullets). Each sense is shortened as the data build makes the dictionary's short meaning, one sense per line (EJDict: notes 《…》, 『』, 〈C〉/〈U〉 and readings removed, cut at the first sense separator `;` `／` `・`, at most 25 Japanese characters; an English definition cut at the first `;` without its final period, at most 40 characters; another native language's gloss or translation: its first sentence, at most 50 Latin or Cyrillic characters (two rows of the card, as Japanese's 25; 2026-11-19); a translation table's sense: its first word; architecture.md "Short lines"). A pointer ("goの過去", "=anyhow", "Plural of …") is followed to the word it names: a form that is the first sense stays first, then that word's first meaning, then the entry's own ("went": "goの過去", "行く,動く", "(ある事をしに)行く"; "found": "findの過去・過去分詞", "(偶然に)…を見つける(進行形にできない)", "…を創設する,設立する(establish)"); an entry with only a reference shows the meanings of the word it names ("anyway" → anyhow's). Left out: senses that come out empty, repeats, and other pointers while a meaning remains. With one sense left the card looks as before. Japanese meanings (EJDict, Wiktionary's translations) and native-language definitions follow this; a meaning that is an English definition translated on the device, or a literal translation, stays one line. The dictionary window, word lists and phrase notes keep the single short meaning
- Buttons: "+ Learning", "Known", "Details" on every hover card (articles and video, 2026-11-14). "Details" opens the dictionary window, as a click on the word does (on articles a click on a word inside a link follows the link, so this is the way in there)
- Phrases: saved phrases and dictionary phrases (Wiktionary idioms, phrasal verbs and prepositional phrases made of vocabulary words, plus EJDict's multi-word headwords) are matched by their words' base forms; the longest match wins (of overlapping saved phrases the longer one, the earlier on a tie) and a saved phrase wins over a detected one, even one that starts earlier. An unsaved detected phrase is New (the known level does not apply to phrases) and does not cover a word saved as Learning. Automatic highlighting decides by meaning, not by how common the words are ([architecture](architecture.md) "Phrase data", 2026-11-13): a phrase whose meaning is not its words' meaning (idioms, discourse markers such as "you know", "of course", "kind of", phrasal verbs) is highlighted; phrases starting with be/have/do, transparent compounds ("digital signature"), named entities ("New York") and phrases whose definitions say what their words say ("come to", "in advance") are left out. They still match when selected or saved, as does every other Wiktionary multi-word entry. Precision before recall (user, 2026-11-13; [architecture](architecture.md) "Phrase precision"): a detected phrase is highlighted only where its context fits its part of speech — not a whole utterance ("to be honest"), not a verb + particle whose particle reads as a preposition ("talk about it", "come to Tokyo"), not "the first thing", "for so long", "take that mask off" — and its words are then highlighted as words
- Phrase patterns ([architecture](architecture.md) "Phrase patterns"): a pattern is a sequence of words and slots (`one's`, `someone`, `something`, `oneself` in English) with flags `detect` (used for automatic highlighting) and `gap` (words may come between its parts, as in phrasal verbs: "figure it out" → `figure out`). A pattern's word matches a token's written form or any of its base forms ("spilled the beans" → `spill the beans`). Automatic highlighting stays strict (contiguous `detect` patterns without slots, the filters above); saved phrases also match with slots and gaps. A phrase with a gap is drawn in parts, and the words in the gap keep their own highlights
- Phrase meanings: EJDict → Wiktionary's Japanese translations → Wiktionary's English definition (marked `lang="en"`). An English definition is translated on the device by the browser's translator when it shows (the definition, not the phrase: "To reveal a secret." rather than "spill the beans"), marked "Machine translation"; a definition that only says how the phrase is used or names its function ("Used to…", "A mild intensifier", "A phrase used at parting") gives way to the phrase's own literal translation, marked the same way ("I mean"); without a translator the English definition shows as it is, and so it does until the translator's model is on the device (Chrome downloads it once, about 20–30 s; the hover card itself never starts the download and looks the same meanwhile: see "Translator model download"). A selection that matches no pattern gets a literal translation from the browser's translator, marked the same way; if that is unavailable too, or does not answer within 1.5 s (its model may still be downloading), the translation line is left out

## Shared: dictionary window (opens when a word is clicked)
- **A popup window floating over the page.** It can be dragged by its handle and closed with ×, Escape, or a press anywhere outside it (a press on another word switches the window to that word instead; a press on the hover card keeps it). It is not a fixed column
  - Video: a window over the right part of the video, its right edge just past the transcript's left edge (at the video's right edge without a transcript), so it covers almost none of the transcript; when subtitles are inside the video, placed so it does not cover them: it ends 60% down the video, at most 420 px tall as in fullscreen and beside the sidebar, at least 240 px (2026-11-16; at 340 px the "In this sentence" card was below the fold)
  - Article: a panel floating on the right side of the screen
- Contents
  - Headword, kind, pronunciation playback (`speechSynthesis`), **IPA**, notes, short translation
  - Status buttons: Learning / Known / Remove ("Not saved" when unsaved)
  - Meanings by part of speech. English definitions are translated on the device (up to four) and marked "Machine translation"; senses from Wiktionary's translation tables show the English sense as a hint before the native words
  - **Translator model download** (2026-11-19; mock `TranslatedSenses`, the left window's two states): while the English definitions wait for the translator's model (to be downloaded or downloading), the Meanings heading says so where "Machine translation" goes, in the same pill (a status): "Downloading translator · 45%" once the browser reports progress, "Downloading translator…" before; the senses and the short meaning show in English meanwhile. Once translated, "Machine translation" takes its place. The download starts at the first click (tap, key) on a page Langle runs on, when the toolbar popup opens, when a language is chosen in Settings, or with the click that opens the window, whichever comes first; never on hover. Nothing shows where the browser has no translator (Firefox) or the model is there
  - External dictionary links: Cambridge / Wiktionary / Reverso / WordReference (each can be turned on/off in settings; **users cannot add their own dictionary URLs**)
  - "In this video" / "On this page": other occurrences in the same video or page (with timestamps for videos; clicking seeks there). In a video's list the term is marked in each line with its category's highlight (as in the transcript, shown even when that category's highlight is switched off; each part of a phrase with a gap). On a page the list has every sentence with the term, the clicked one included, as in the mock; clicking one scrolls to it
  - "Seen elsewhere": occurrences in other sources. For unsaved words, explain that saving starts recording
- "In this sentence" (phase 3, built 2026-11-16): see "Meaning in context and your meanings". No "Ask AI" links to ChatGPT / Claude (user, 2026-11-15)

## Video UI (YouTube / Netflix)
- **Two columns**: video on the left, transcript (subtitle cards) on the right. The dictionary is the popup window above
- **Transcript sidebar** (mock `Netflix — transcript beside the video` and `Fullscreen`, 2026-11-13): where the player fills the window or the screen (Netflix in the window and in fullscreen, YouTube in fullscreen) the player gets narrower and the column sits to its right as a sidebar: 360 px wide, 16 px from the top, right and bottom edges, 1 px border, 12 px corners. The player takes the rest (window − 360 − 2 × 16), the video letterboxed in it; the site's control bar and back arrow shrink with it. Nothing of Langle's covers the video. The dictionary window opens over the player with its right edge at the sidebar's left edge, 72 px from the top, 360 × 420, with the darker shadow. The sidebar is not there while Langle is off, for a video without subtitles, or while the column is closed: the player then has the whole width. Where the site's player cannot be narrowed (a page Langle does not recognize), there is no sidebar
- **Closing the column**: × "Close transcript" at the top right of the column header (after the Transcript / Words tabs), on every page that has the column, closes the whole column (both tabs). The menu's "Show transcript in sidebar" switch brings it back. Both are one saved setting (on by default) for every page (YouTube watch page, Netflix, fullscreen), in the menu only, not on the settings page. On a YouTube watch page with the column closed, YouTube's own right column (recommended videos) shows
- **Subtitle position is selectable**: "In video" (overlaid on the video, default) / "Below video" (a large box under the video)
- **Netflix's control bar is held back** (2026-11-14) while Langle's subtitle is over the video: Netflix shows its bar on any pointer move and the subtitle rises above it, so moving to a word would move the word. The bar (with the back arrow and flag at the top) shows only once the pointer has stayed in the bar's area at the bottom (120 px) for 1 s, moving or not, or at once when the pointer is in the back arrow's strip at the top (80 px); moves anywhere else never show it (user, 2026-11-14: the 1.5 s of moves over the video before brought it on the way to words). Leaving the area (or the window) before the second is up starts over. After the pointer rests and Netflix takes the bar away, the next one is held back again. Ending Langle's use with the pointer in the bar's area starts the second then. It never shows while Langle is in use (see "Pausing while Langle is in use"). With Langle off or the subtitle below the video, the bar shows as Netflix shows it
- **Translation visibility is set separately for the subtitle and the transcript** (both shown by default)
- **Prefer translations provided by the site** (order in [architecture](architecture.md) "Choosing a translation"). Where a translation came from is not shown
- **Two buttons in the player bar**, grouped in **one outline (translucent white)** over a faint white tint (2026-11-14) so they are not confused with YouTube's own gear
  - **L button** (Langle's mark, 26 px, in a 36 px button; 2026-11-18): one click turns Langle on/off (when off: the mark in gray, as the toolbar's off icon, inside a dashed outline). The state is remembered per platform (YouTube, Netflix): the next video there starts the same way
  - **Up arrow (^) button**: opens the Langle menu (the ^ button itself has no gear icon). From the top:
    - Header: L, Langle, current state (On / Off for this video), and a gear at the right that opens the settings page ("Open settings")
    - "Show transcript in sidebar" (switch): the column beside the video (see "Closing the column")
    - "Text size": a slider for the subtitle's and its translation's text size, 50–200% in steps of 10 (default 100%), in every subtitle position and in fullscreen
    - "Background": a slider for how dark the box behind the subtitle and translation over the video is, 0–100% in steps of 5 (default 80%; the translation's box is 0.9 of that). The light box below the video keeps its look
    - "Show dual subtitles" (switch): the subtitle's translation under it
    - "Subtitle position" as a label (14 px, sentence case, like "Text size") on its own line, with the In video / Below video choice under it at full width, the last row: an outlined box (1 px border, 8 px corners), 14 px text, the chosen option filled with the green accent and white text
    - The sliders show their value on the subtitle while dragged and are saved when let go
- Transcript
  - Each card shows the original with the translation below it. The playing card is emphasized and auto-scrolled; clicking a card seeks there and plays (a paused video starts)
  - The auto-scroll waits while the pointer is over the list or a press that began in it is held (2026-11-19: a card moving under the pointer took the press); the pointer coming onto the list, or a press, stops a scroll under way. It catches up to the playing card when the pointer leaves
  - Words in a card work as in the subtitle: hovering one shows its hover card, clicking it opens the dictionary window (no seek). The card goes when the list scrolls, and while it shows the playing card does not scroll the list
  - Each card is a **sentence** of the track's cues, never part of a cue (2026-11-15): manual tracks (YouTube's and Netflix's) go through the same sentence rules as auto-generated ones below, each cue's time spread over its characters, but a card boundary only falls between cues, so the subtitle in the video still shows one whole cue at a time. A sentence ends after . ? ! … ♪ (maybe before a closing quote); a sentence that ends inside a cue goes on to the cue's end. Sound labels ("[MUSIC]") stay alone; a cue starting with a speaker's dash ("- …") starts a card, and a cue with two speakers' lines stays one card, each dash line keeping its own line in the card's text ("- How much?\n- Two hundred pounds."). A ♪ that opens a line starts a sentence and does not end the one before. A sentence over 30 words is split as few times as needed, between cues, at its likeliest boundary (a clause mark such as a comma) near an even share. With YouTube's auto-translation, the cards follow its sentences as for auto-generated tracks (below; YouTube translates a manual track sentence by sentence too), between cues only. With a human translation track, adjacent sentences that one translated line covers at least half of each are one card (up to 30 words), so the line is not shown on both
  - Auto-generated (ASR) tracks come as rolling fragments with word timings, often without punctuation. With YouTube's auto-translation, the cards follow its sentences: YouTube translates whole sentences and spreads them over the fragments, so each sentence end in the translation (。！？, or . ! ? before a space; not inside a bracket pair that closes within 20 characters, such as 「いいね！」: a longer quotation's sentences end inside it, and a bracket that never closes ends nothing) marks the nearest likely boundary in the original (a sentence end, a speaker change ">>", a comma, a pause, or an English sentence starter such as "so" or "I"), and each card gets exactly the translated sentences between its ends. Each end is timed when it is said: a translated piece's time is spread over its characters, so a piece holding several sentences dates each end where it falls (2026-11-15). A speaker change (">>") always ends a card (2026-11-15), also inside one translated sentence (YouTube runs two speakers into one): the translation is cut at the clause end (、 , ;) said closest to the change, within 3 s and only between two clauses; with none near, each clause goes to the side it ends on, so a card can be left without a translation (a "[laughter]" card before ">> Live singing…?"). A card holds at most 30 words (2026-11-14): a longer one (YouTube translates a run-on as one sentence) is split into as few even parts as that takes, each at the likeliest boundary near an even share, and its translation at the clause end (、 , ;) said closest to that boundary; with no clause end there, each part shows the whole translation, as rule G does. Without the auto-translation, a sentence ends at . ? !, sound labels and ">>", plus pauses of 0.8 s in tracks without punctuation, up to 20 words (30 in punctuated tracks). In the video an ASR card shows as lines of at most 60 characters (30 for languages written without spaces), split where the halves balance best, preferring pauses and punctuation; the card's whole translation stays under each line
  - Header: "Highlight" category toggles and the transcript translation switch
  - **While the language's data is missing** (mock `LanguageData`): one line at the top of the column, "Downloading Spanish data · 45%" over a bar, or "Couldn't download Spanish data" with Retry. The cards show without highlights and are classified once the data is there; the line then goes. The data pages need comes first: the line goes before the pair's fallback (the English definitions) is there ([architecture](architecture.md) "Distribution": order and readiness), unless the fallback fails
- **Selecting words** (2026-11-13): selecting 2–8 words in the subtitle (in every position, in fullscreen and over a Short) or in a transcript card's original works as on articles ("Article UI" below): the selection snaps to whole words and shows the same card with "Selected · not saved", over the selected words; words partly over a highlighted phrase are saved as selected, as on articles. Over video this card also has "Details" (a selection cannot be clicked), which opens the dictionary window for the phrase. "+ Learning" / "Known" save the phrase; the card and the selection then go and the phrase is highlighted. A selection stays within one subtitle line or one card
  - **Langle draws the selection** (2026-11-19): a press on the text selects nothing by itself; once it has moved a few pixels the selection grows from the pressed word to the word nearest the pointer, whole words, as the drag goes. Past the line's or the card's edge (over the video, the page, another card) it keeps to that text's nearest word, so the drag never selects the site's page, the site never gets its click (no play or pause), and the transcript does not jump. A press that does not move is a click (the word opens)
  - The words may be separated by punctuation ("WH-WHAT", "Well, I think"; 2026-11-19); the phrase is saved with what separates them (key `wh-what`, `well, i think`) and is highlighted where the same words come with the same punctuation
  - From the pressed word a drag takes at most 8 words: dragged further, the selection stops growing. One word opens its dictionary window, as a click on it does. Anything that makes no card leaves nothing selected and the video is not held
  - The video pauses once the drag starts (see "Pausing while Langle is in use"), not on the press
- **Pausing while Langle is in use** (2026-11-13; the subtitle itself 2026-11-14): the video pauses as soon as the pointer is on the subtitle (its lines over the video, or the box below the video; not the transcript), when a selection starts in the subtitle or the transcript, when a word's hover card shows, and while the dictionary window is open. Always on, no setting. When the last of these closes, the video plays again, only if Langle paused it and the user did not press play or pause in the meantime. While one of these is open, Netflix's control bar does not show and the subtitle over the video stays where it is (the word under the pointer would move away and close the hover card); it moves once the last closes
- No instructions text under the video
- **When the video ends**, nothing is shown; unsaved New words are handled as Settings say (see "Unsaved New words at the end" below)

## YouTube comments (mock `YouTube — comments`, 2026-11-12)
- On watch pages, the comments and replies under the video (`ytd-comments#comments`) work like an article: highlights, the article hover card (with Details), selecting 2–8 words to save a phrase, and the dictionary panel 16px in from the window's top right. Only comment text counts: names, times, buttons, the header and the comment box get nothing
- Each comment's language is detected on its own (`<html lang>` is YouTube's interface language): a comment the detector reliably says is in another language is left alone; one it cannot decide on counts as the learning language. A comment YouTube translates is checked again
- Records are kept as on articles: a word saved from a comment keeps the comment's sentence, saved words seen in comments add occurrences, and comment sentences go into the sentence history. The source is the video (`platform` youtube, its watch URL and title) with the sentence as the position, so "Seen elsewhere" reads "YouTube · Nov 12" and links to the video. The Words tab, the popup's "This video" and the end-of-video save count subtitles only
- Only one dictionary is open at a time: opening the comments panel closes the video's window, and opening the video's window closes the panel
- Follows Settings → Sites → "YouTube comments" (on by default; needs YouTube on) and the popup's "On this site", not the player's L button. It covers Shorts' comments too (below)

## YouTube Shorts (mock `YouTube Shorts — subtitles` and `— comments`, 2026-11-13)
- Shorts (`/shorts/<id>`) get the subtitle only: Langle's subtitle and its translation over the vertical video, above the Short's channel and title, with the hover card and the dictionary window as on a watch page. No transcript and no "Below video": the ^ menu is Text size, Background and Show dual subtitles (no transcript switch, no "Subtitle position"), and the settings stay as they are for watch pages
- The subtitle is a little smaller than on a watch page (20 px and 15 px for the translation at 100%); "Text size" and "Background" apply as usual
- The L and ^ group sits in the Short's own controls at the top of the video, before the captions button; the menu opens downward
- The dictionary window opens beside the video, right of YouTube's like / comments buttons, so it covers none of the video (over the comments panel when that is open)
- Moving to the next or previous Short loads its subtitles. A Short loops instead of ending: its first loop counts as the end for "Unsaved New words at the end"
- The comments panel (opened with the comments button) works like the comments under a watch video (above). Its dictionary panel opens just left of the comments panel, over the video, so the comments stay readable. The panel stays open when moving to the next Short, and Langle starts over on the new comments
- Records: the source is the video's watch URL (`watch?v=<id>`) and the Short's title

## Article UI (content script)
- Detect the page language (`<html lang>` → `i18n.detectLanguage`) and run only when it matches the learning language
- While the language's data is missing (downloaded packs), the page shows nothing; it is analyzed once the data is there (the popup and Settings show the download)
- A new learning language applies to open pages without a reload (2026-11-19): a page in that language starts (it is analyzed once its data is there), and a page in the old one loses its highlights and cards; a new native language changes the cards' meanings at the next hover
- Color by category with the **CSS Custom Highlight API** (no DOM rewriting). `::highlight()` cannot draw padding, rounded corners or box shadows, so the mock's bottom bar is drawn as a 2px underline and the Learning background covers only the text height — the closest the API allows. Process only the visible range, progressively, and follow page changes with a MutationObserver
  - In browsers where highlights cannot be underlined, use background colors only (see [architecture](architecture.md) "Browser differences")
  - The text of a word with a background (New, Learning) is drawn in the mock's ink (`#14182B`): the backgrounds are light, and a dark page's white text (YouTube's dark theme) would vanish on them
- Find the hovered word with `caretPositionFromPoint`
- When 2–8 words are selected, snap the range to word boundaries and show the same card as a hovered phrase, where that card would sit, with the status "Selected · not saved" (mock PhraseMeaning): it names the phrase pattern the selection matched with its IPA, short meaning and main word, and saving uses the pattern's key ("made up her mind" is saved as `make up one's mind`). Only a pattern that spans the whole selection counts; the sentence decides when the selection is part of a longer pattern ("made up" → `make up one's mind`). Otherwise the selected words themselves are the phrase, even when some of them are already highlighted (2026-11-14): a selection that reaches past a highlighted phrase, or covers only part of one, shows and saves the selected words. A selection that matches no pattern shows the selected words with their literal translation and the "Machine translation" mark. "+ Learning" / "Known" save the phrase; the card and the selection then go and the phrase is highlighted, in place of a detected phrase it overlaps (a saved phrase wins over a detected one even when the detected one starts earlier) and of a shorter saved phrase it overlaps (between two saved phrases of the same length, words from first to last, the earlier one stays). A press inside the selected words starts a new selection rather than dragging them. "Details" opens the dictionary panel for the phrase ("Not saved" until saved from there). Hover cards do not show while the selection card does. Phrases saved earlier keep their keys
  - Punctuation may separate the words (2026-11-19, as over video): the phrase is saved with what separates them ("well, i think") and highlighted where the same words come with the same punctuation. A line break may not
  - One selected word (a double-click, a drag inside a word) opens its dictionary panel, as a click on it does. More than 8 words, or words across blocks, show no card and the page's selection stays as the browser made it (it may be for copying): on a page Langle does not draw the selection, so it does not cut it to 8
- No card sits on the page (highlight toggles live in the toolbar popup). The dictionary panel sits 16px in from the viewport's top right (the mock's 84px makes room for its own page header)
- The clicked word gets the mock's green outline (drawn by Langle's UI over the page, since `::highlight()` cannot draw outlines)
- **Only the title and body** (Settings → Articles → "Highlight only the article", on by default): the main content gets highlights, the end-of-article save (below), the sentence history and "Seen elsewhere" encounters. Menus, sidebars, footers and links to other stories get none of these, but hovering or clicking a word there works as anywhere. Switched off, the whole page counts
  - The body's container: paragraphs of prose (not list items, table cells, headings or captions; at least 25 characters outside links, less than half link text) score their parent fully, grandparent by 1/2 and great-grandparent by 1/3; the best-scored element, widened to its nearest `article`, `main` or `[role=main]`
  - The body ends with its last part: the best-scored element and the elements in the container scoring at least 1/4 as well (the second half of a body split by an ad slot) are its parts, and the smallest part around the last of their paragraphs is the end. What follows it in the container is left out (author bios, sign-up and login walls, donation asks, cards for other stories); what comes before the body stays (title, standfirst, byline, lead image caption)

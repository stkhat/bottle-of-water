## Known issues and open points

### Video
- Netflix's Japanese lines are loosely timed, so a few land on a neighboring transcript card; where a site's translation is cut finer than the cards and timed loosely, a short card left without its own line can now show its neighbour's (rule G); sound labels inside a cue stay in the card ("… ( chuckles )")
- Transcript sidebar in real fullscreen not checked in the user's Chrome: Chrome refuses fullscreen to Claude in Chrome's clicks ("not granted"); the narrowing itself was seen working on YouTube in fullscreen during the investigation, and browser tests cover real fullscreen on the fixture pages
- Netflix: the ^ menu goes away with Netflix's control bar when the pointer stops (it shows again with the bar)
- YouTube: real-site layout in theater mode and the narrow single-column layout not checked
- YouTube Shorts: ASR lines are split for the watch page's width (up to 60 characters), so a long one wraps into two or three lines over the narrow video; the dictionary window covers the comments panel while that is open
- YouTube: after using L or ^ the focus stays on Langle's buttons, whose event isolation keeps YouTube's shortcuts (f, k) from reaching the player until the video is clicked
- YouTube ASR: where the English has no punctuation, a card boundary can be 1–3 words off (YouTube's own translation drifts). Where YouTube translates one English sentence as two, the card holds both, or the English is split at a comma. A card split for length (over 30 words) cuts its translation at the clause end said nearest the split; Japanese word order puts some clauses on the other part ("DDR5メモリの現在の価格を考えると、" under the English before it), and 7 of 199 split cards in the corpus show the whole translation on each part (no clause end there). A card cut at a speaker change ">>" cuts its translation the same way; 2% of round 2's cards show no translation (mostly sound labels such as "[laughter]" before a ">>", some short replies whose translation YouTube put after the change)
- Manual tracks: a card never splits a cue, so where the track's sentences end inside its cues a card ends at a cue's end instead: 10–20% of YouTube cards end mid-sentence with the next going on in lower case (Netflix 0.2%). With YouTube's translation, 0.1–0.4% of whole-sentence cards show a Japanese cut mid-sentence (was 11%)
- YouTube Shorts: once, a full load with the user's caption language set to Japanese made no caption request at all, also not after Langle asked the player for the English track ([notes](notes/youtube-shorts.md)); not seen again. Possibly the class fixed on 2026-11-19 (a track already on gets no request when set again)
- Subtitles: the outline of the term whose dictionary window is open goes around the clicked part of a gap phrase only
- Video lines that play while the language data is missing are classified later but not kept in the sentence history

### Meaning in context
- In YouTube comments the lines around the sentence are the comment's other sentences and the replies YouTube has loaded: a one-sentence comment with its replies closed is explained from its sentence alone
- 2026-11-16: the reading placeholder says "Thinking…", and "Starting on-device AI…" only while the model itself has been starting for more than 1 s (the page's base session being created); the card's footer reads "On-device AI" without a line count. The slow start is covered by tests only: it cannot be brought about on demand in the user's Chrome
- Deferred (user, 2026-11-16): hiding the first explanation's delay (about 10 s after the model was idle, nearly all of it the base session's `create()`). Two ways, neither built now: create the page's base session ahead of time when a page loads (costs memory and power on every page where no explanation is asked), or share one base session across pages from an offscreen document (it runs prompts as fast as a content script, M0 check C2, but it cannot start a download, which needs a click on the page)
- Explanations dropped (user, 2026-11-16, [notes](notes/meaning-in-context.md) "When to show an explanation"): the card shows the meaning alone, and the prompt asks for the marked words only. Meanings that take in words around the term are fewer (38% → 32% judged) but not gone ("we can't grasp" → 理解できない is the kind left). "Fits here" fit 5 of 6 terms (M0: about 3 in 4)

### YouTube comments
- Short comments ("lol", "wow") are often not decided by the language detector and count as the learning language
- A comment cut off by "Read more" is analyzed whole; its hidden part gets highlights once expanded

### Articles
- A word saved in another tab shows on an open page only after it is classified again (reload or a known-level change)
- Wikipedia sentences keep citation brackets ("5] …", "…[") in "On this page" and the history
- A page whose first analysis ends just as the language pair changes can log "No dictionary from en to en": `warmDictionary` pairs the page's language with the new native language (a rare E2E flake in `extension-errors.spec.ts`)
- On an article or video whose language pack is there but whose dictionary is not (still downloading, or failed), hovering a word shows nothing and logs nothing; since 2026-11-18 the hover card (and a window clicked meanwhile) shows once the data is there, while the pointer is still on the word. If the lookup's "data missing" answer comes back after the data became ready, it still waits for the next change (the pack wait's guard against asking again on the same states)

### Popup, word list, history, sync
- Popup: "On this site" reflects only `disabledSites` (a "Never run on" host still shows the switch on); relative times read "2 minutes ago" where the mock says "2 min ago"
- Word list: re-saving a removed word brings back one occurrence (removal deletes the others)
- A saved phrase only finds history sentences recorded after it was saved (index keys are fixed when a sentence is kept)
- The 50 MB history figure is Langle's own estimate; IndexedDB's indexes take more space on disk
- `storage.sync` holds about 12,400 English terms; demotion of "save all" terms keeps it working

### Phrases and language data
- Some first meanings from Wiktionary fit badly ("used to" → "accustomed to"; "feel like" → ほしい for "I feel like we should"; "come in" → "To become relevant"; "get around" → moving round an obstacle, Wiktionary's first sense: its labels do not tell the literal sense apart reliably, see architecture "C2"); a few literal readings of idioms remain highlighted ("take it", "get it done", "give me reason", "play games"), and "a lot" and "thank you" are highlighted though learners know them (low value)
- Phrase data (B2): definitions that paraphrase their words leave useful phrases to selections ("as soon as", "in advance", "in particular", "may as well", "first thing"); B2b also takes some useful compounds for transparent (prime minister, blood vessel, civil service, real estate); B4 leaves out phrases written in Title Case in Wiktionary ("Your Royal Highness"). The judged useful share of highlights went from 94% to 79% while their number nearly tripled (precision before recall: the rules P1, P2 and the context checks apply on top)
- `en/phrases.json` (73,112 patterns) is 431 KB gzipped, loaded with every English page; the dictionary (`en-ja` with `en-defs`) parses in ~120 ms instead of ~85 ms in Node
- Phrase precision: the tagger reads some adverbial particles as prepositions, so P2 also drops useful phrasal verbs ("fill in the form", "stand by", "look after", "lean into"); literal vs idiomatic readings with the same tags stay ("go off", "cut out", "in time"); unpunctuated speech recognition loses a few prepositional phrases before a noun ("in particular transport")
- A saved phrasal verb may match words that do not belong together (up to three words between its parts; the phrase rules check detected phrases only)
- A phrase saved before M9a whose key contains a slot word ("someone else") also matches the slot's fillers ("him else")
- Machine-translated definitions: about 70 of 100 read well in Chrome's en→ja; fragments and jargon translate badly, so the window keeps the English under each sense. The first translation needs Chrome's model, whose download needs a click; until then hover cards show English
- Native editions' glosses are of mixed kinds (a word, a list, or a whole definition); translation-table senses can be rare words. Phrases are covered far less than words (es 24.7%, pt 17.7%)
- English → Spanish and Portuguese (2026-11-18, rule A1 in [architecture](specs/architecture.md) "Tags"): a form the pair dictionary has no entry for shows its base's entry (told → tell, "has a dog" → have, children → child), and an auxiliary whose pointer names a word that is not mainly a verb goes to its verb (does → do). Over the 171,709-token corpus, shown words with no hover went es 2,635 → 2,074 and pt 3,386 → 2,286; Japanese is unchanged. Still without a hover: auxiliaries without an entry ("has been", "did you": es 225, pt 427 tokens), since U1-2 and 2026-11-19 shown their own pointer or their base; contractions (I'm, I've) and capitalised words (British, Thursday)
- CORE fixes the Spanish study exposed (2026-11-18, [architecture](specs/architecture.md) "Tags" and "Phrase precision"; [notes](notes/spanish-quality.md) "Suggested order" step 1): A1 for auxiliaries that are no headwords, A1b, BASE, LONGEST and Unicode word shapes. English: 63 hovers and 25 of 4,584 highlights change (judged a small gain: 12 wrong matches gone, 10 useful highlights lost, such as "in love" inside "fall in love"). Spanish changes only once its tagger and phrase parts of speech ship (steps 2 and 5).
- Spanish tagger and grammar (U2-2, 2026-11-18; [architecture](specs/architecture.md) "Tags", "Vocabulary and frequency"; [notes](notes/spanish-quality.md) "Suggested order" step 2): `es` 2026.1118.0 (format 2) adds `tagger.json` (UD Spanish AnCora + GSD, 97 KB gzipped) and `pos.json`, lists a form's bases by the treebanks' lemmas (creo → creer), and gives a form-of sense's meaning after its pointer ("les" → "to them, for them"); `es-en` 2026.1118.0. Over the 72,828-token Spanish corpus, 17,072 hovers change (de → of, es → ser, los → el, me → "me"); judged blind on a fresh sample, good hovers YouTube 64 → 79%, articles 62 → 78%, Netflix (held out) 62 → 73%, wrong or misleading 17 → 3%, 13 → 3%, 18 → 8%. Still wrong: "hace" (ago) and "¿vale?" read as verbs, "lo" before "que" / an adjective shows the pronoun first, ambiguous forms the tag cannot settle (ven, fue). Not checked in the user's Chrome; the new versions are not on npm yet `en/tagger.json` is kept as trained with ASCII shapes (same accuracy); the next English data rebuild retrains it
- Edition forms as pointers (U1-2, 2026-11-18; [architecture](specs/architecture.md) "Edition forms", "Pair pointers"): every recipe pair keeps its Wiktionary edition's form-of senses as pointers, in the syntax learned from that edition. In a spaced script a verb form shows its base's meaning first and its grammar second (told → contar, "Pasado simple del verbo tell"); auxiliaries ("has been" → "Tercera persona … de have", tener), comparatives and plurals show the pointer first; Chinese and Japanese show every pointer first. Shown words with no hover: es 2,074 → 1,847, pt 2,286 → 1,858, fr 2,344 → 2,058, de 2,286 → 1,913, zh 2,484 → 2,419, it 2,486 → 2,283, pl 2,434 → 2,064, ru unchanged (2,250), ja unchanged. Judged blind (es, pt, fr, ru, Netflix held out): on changed words, wrong, misleading and missing hovers drop by about two thirds and meaning-first is chosen best far more often than the pointer-first rule or U1-1 (es 37 vs 18 vs 12; [notes](notes/native-quality.md) "Suggested order"). Still open: contraction expansions in pl (that's → "that is / that has"), auxiliaries' long pointer lines (over the 50 cap: es 565 lines)
- Capitalised headwords in every recipe pair (U1-3, 2026-11-19; pair format 3; [architecture](specs/architecture.md) "Capitalised headwords of the recipe pairs"): each pair keeps its edition's and the translation tables' capitalised headwords apart in `capital.json` (Japan → Japón, January → enero, Thursday → jueves, I'm, British, US → EE. UU.), read as written; what each means ("name", "word", none) is decided at build time from the native edition's own entries, class glosses (Apellido ×120) left out, persons' "word" meanings dropped. Names es 1,496 → 1,310, fr → 1,218, zh → 1,040 (ja 1,051, unchanged); shown words with no hover es 1,847 → 126, pt 1,858 → 69, fr 2,058 → 442, de 1,913 → 267, ru 2,250 → 1,504, zh 2,419 → 847, it 2,283 → 699, pl 2,064 → 1,093. - CORE hover fixes from the 2026-11-18 real-site check (2026-11-19; [architecture](specs/architecture.md) "Tags"): an auxiliary without an entry goes to its base instead of an empty card (en-ru/zh/it "has been" → have): the base's senses its source calls auxiliary where it has some (en-it → avere, essere), else no meaning, only the base's head, pronunciation and the grammar line "has → have" (ru, zh, de "does"); degrees an edition leaves unmarked are read as pointers and, after a verb's or a degree's form, the base's meanings of that part of speech come before the word's own of another (es better/JJ → "Comparativo irregular de good", bueno, mejorar); a phrase card's word line uses the word's tag and is left out when the entry has no sense of that part of speech (pt "in other words" no longer notes the verb "to other"). - The gate's word list still counts some names EJDict writes in lowercase (billy, phoebe) and forms the lemma data lacks (gotten, ladies, nights)
- Tags (rules A1, A2): tagger errors move a few words to the wrong entry or senses (food coloring → color, "Left, right" → leave, "people dedicated to" → dedicate, gaming machines → game); "following X" (a preposition) shows follow's verb senses, as EJDict's "following" has no preposition sense; the light verbs' bases show their first senses (made → …を作る); EJDict's 〈C〉/ending heuristics mislabel a few senses (better: いっそうよく as a verb). - Vocabulary (rule E): the first definition of some new words is the wrong sense (cuz → "A cousin", kinda → a baboon, aka → a vine; the tagger does not move them); capitalised abbreviations get the lowercase word's definitions (Nov. → "A novice"); a name the names lexicon lacks as a given name stays a word (Ted → "A Teddy boy"). ## Before publishing (Chrome Web Store)
- Privacy policy page on a public URL (this repo is private). It and the listing say the language data is downloaded from jsDelivr / unpkg right after install (no pack is bundled; [architecture](specs/architecture.md) "Chrome Web Store requirements")
- Trader / non-trader declaration, 2-Step Verification on the developer account
- Review justifications: `<all_urls>`, page hooks on YouTube/Netflix, subtitle handling, sentence history described in the listing ([architecture](specs/architecture.md) "Chrome Web Store requirements")
- On-device AI (phase 3) in the store listing and the privacy policy: "Explain in context" sends the term, its sentence and up to 5 lines around it, the page's or video's title and the dictionary's meanings to Chrome's built-in model on the device; nothing leaves the device, and the answers are kept only on the device (never exported, never synced). Terms without a dictionary entry are explained as soon as the dictionary window opens unless Settings turns it off. Acknowledge Google's Generative AI Prohibited Uses Policy as the developer (no API or manifest step; [notes](notes/meaning-in-context.md) C1)
